Section 1 · What actually builds muscle

Before you pick up a dumbbell or plan a single meal, get clear on the handful of things that genuinely drive results. No fluff, no debates over terminology — just the mechanisms.

Progressive Overload: Building muscle requires getting stronger over time — there's no way around it. If your lifts haven't improved after 3-6 months of steady training, meaningful muscle gain is very unlikely. Track strength because it's measurable; muscle grows far too slowly to judge reliably in the mirror.

Intensity: Work each set until the weight visibly slows even though you're pushing hard — that's the sign you've created enough stimulus. Pick a load that's a genuine challenge within the target rep range. (Just starting out? Stopping 1-2 reps short of the point where good form breaks down is close enough to failure to grow.)

Good technique: Never trade form for a heavier weight. Every rep should look the same from first to last, through a full range of motion — the only thing that should change by the final rep is the speed, since you naturally slow down as you approach failure.

Sleep: Six hours is the floor, seven is adequate, eight or more is ideal. The gap between 5-6 and 7-8 hours a night is enough to be the difference between stalled progress and the physique you're after.

Enough protein: 0.8-1g per pound of bodyweight works for almost everyone. Carrying a lot of extra bodyfat? Base it on your goal weight instead (at 300lb aiming for 180lb → 150-180g).

Enough calories: Underweight? Eat 100-200 over maintenance. Overweight? 300-500 under. Everyone else: eat at maintenance and make sure protein is covered.

Finding maintenance: Trial and error beats any formula — eat the same calories every day, weigh in at the same time each day, and watch the trend line. An online calculator gets you close enough to start.

Consistency: The boring truth. Even with perfect adherence you won't see change day to day. Trust the process, keep showing up, and the physiology guarantees your results.

Section 2 · The Upper Lower Split

How to properly warm up (5–10 min)

Before every exercise: take 70% of your normal weight (normally press 50 lb dumbbells? take the 35s) and do 1 "feeler" set of 4-5 reps with excellent technique, then start your working sets.

The feeler set doesn't count toward working sets. 2x6-10 means feeler + 2 working sets.

Why Upper Lower? Flexible scheduling while still training every muscle frequently. Miles has used this exact setup for 5 years with more gains than any other split.

You train strength on Monday, Wednesday and Friday. The four workouts below cycle through those slots in order (Upper A → Lower A → Upper B → Lower B), so week 1 is Upper A / Lower A / Upper B and week 2 picks up with Lower B — just do the next workout in the sequence each strength day.

How to choose your weight & progressively overload

Example: incline dumbbell press.

1) Pick a weight you struggle with at 8-12 reps — at LEAST 8, but not 13 without form breakdown. Say 50 lb dumbbells and you barely get 10 — excellent.
2) Next session, push for 11 reps.
3) Session after, push for 12.
4) Hit 12 safely? Move up (55 lb) and start over at 8.
5) Repeat forever. That's the whole process.

You'll misjudge weights at first — that's fine. Only 6 reps, or overshot to 15? Adjust next set or session. Some sessions you won't get the extra rep; some you'll get two. Stay consistent and never sacrifice form for a higher number.

Missed a day?

Don't stress — Miles has had months missing a day each week and still progressed. It's a long-term project.

Option A: shift the session to the next available day / swap with a rest day.
Option B: skip it and continue as programmed.

Best case: missed Tuesday's Lower? Do it Wednesday and shift Thursday/Friday back a day. Can't shift? Do the leg day Wednesday and resume as normal — expect Friday's legs to be tougher (drop weights 10-20% if needed).

Worst case: only twice in the gym this week? Do Upper A and Lower A. Two sessions beats zero and one imperfect week won't hurt.

Section 3 · Couch to 5K

Why do cardio at all?

Skipping cardio and just eating less might work calorically, but you'd miss out on:

  • Improved cardiovascular capacity → better lifting performance
  • Reduced blood pressure · improved metabolic function
  • Improved insulin sensitivity — muscles more receptive to food
  • Reduced resting heart rate → more restorative sleep

Section 4 · So what now?

That was a lot to take in — don't let it freeze you up. Nothing here needs memorising; your only job is to turn up on Monday.

Sunday: rest — shop for groceries (protein, carbs, fats) and get a proper night's sleep

Monday: strength — Upper A — warm up properly, find your starting weights

Tuesday: Couch to 5K

Wednesday: strength — Lower A

Thursday: Couch to 5K

Friday: strength — Upper B

Saturday: Couch to 5K

Next week your strength days carry on from where you left off — Monday is Lower B, then the cycle starts over with Upper A.

Perfection isn't the goal — consistency is, as much of it as you can manage. Turn up on Monday, run the program, get your protein in, and sleep well.
