The Level-Up Guide

Four strength workouts, a nine-phase Couch to 5K, and the small set of numbers that make both work. Every exercise is illustrated, with tempo cues, starting weights and a video.

The problem was never information

Everyone already knows roughly what to do: lift weights, eat protein, sleep. Knowing it has built precisely zero muscle.

What actually happens is a burst of motivation, an over-complicated programme, three good weeks. Then one missed session becomes a missed week, and six months later the only thing that's changed is the date. Run that cycle a few times and "I can't stick to anything" starts to feel like a personality trait rather than a planning failure.

So the plan here is deliberately small: four gym sessions rotating through three strength days, three short runs, and a handful of numbers to hit. Few enough moving parts that a bad week can't derail it, and no session ever starts with guesswork, because every exercise comes with an illustration, a starting weight and a video.

How to read the illustration sheets

Each exercise has 4 panels: setup, the two movement phases, and key cues. Speed is marked with pills:

SOLID = powerful, ~1 secDASHED = slow, 2–3 secDARK = pause / hold

Rule of thumb: be explosive on the effort, fight gravity on the way back: the slow lowering is where most growth happens.

This page documents its author's own training and is published as a personal reference. It isn't coaching, a programme recommendation, or medical advice for anyone else. If anything here is useful to you, adapt it to your own health and circumstances first.

Section 1 · The fundamentals

Eight levers decide whether training works. Get them roughly right and almost any sensible programme succeeds; miss them and no programme can compensate.

Get stronger, or nothing is happening

Muscle tracks strength. If the weights you lift creep up across months, muscle is coming with them; if they've been flat for half a year, it isn't, however hard the sessions felt. Keep a log: the logbook is the progress report, because the mirror updates far too slowly to be one.

Take sets close to the limit

A set counts when the weight visibly slows near the end despite an honest push. Finishing with five easy reps in reserve is a warm-up, whatever the programme calls it. New to lifting? Stopping one or two reps before form falls apart is close enough to grow.

Make every rep identical

Choose a full range of motion and defend it. The tenth rep should look exactly like the first, only slower. The moment added weight forces the range to shrink or momentum to creep in, that's load you can't actually use yet.

Treat sleep as part of the programme

Growth happens between sessions, not during them. At 5-6 hours a night recovery lags badly enough to cancel out good training; seven works; eight or more is where you recover fastest. No supplement comes close.

Hit a protein number daily

Roughly 2g per kilo of bodyweight covers nearly everyone. Carrying a lot of body fat? Count from your goal weight instead: aiming for 80 kg means about 160g a day.

Point your calories in one direction

To lose weight: Keep daily cals to around 500 below. Keep the protein number covered, and let training do the reshaping.

Measure maintenance, don't guess it

Eat the same calories daily for a couple of weeks and weigh in at the same time each morning. A rising trend means surplus; a falling one means deficit. A calculator only supplies the first guess; the trend line is the answer.

Keep showing up when nothing visible happens

Even a perfect week changes nothing you can see; results arrive on a delay measured in months. The plan works for the people still running it in month four. That is the entire trick.

Section 2 · The Upper Lower Split

How to properly warm up (5–10 min)

Before every exercise: load roughly 70% of your working weight (press the 20 kg dumbbells? warm up with the 14s, or the nearest pair below) and run one crisp "feeler" set of 4-5 technically perfect reps before the real sets begin.

Feeler sets are extra: a 2x6-10 prescription means the feeler first, then 2 working sets.

Why Upper Lower? Every muscle gets trained often, sessions stay a manageable length, and the rotation forgives an imperfect week: a missed workout never disappears, it just moves to the next slot.

You train strength on Monday, Wednesday and Friday. The four workouts below cycle through those slots in order (Upper A → Lower A → Upper B → Lower B), so week 1 is Upper A / Lower A / Upper B and week 2 picks up with Lower B. Just do the next workout in the sequence each strength day.

Picking weights and moving up

Worked example: incline dumbbell press, target 8-12 reps.

  1. Find the load where 10 reps is a genuine fight but rep 8 is guaranteed with clean form. If the 20 kg dumbbells give you a hard-won 10, that's your working weight.
  2. Each session, try to beat your logbook by a single rep.
  3. The day you reach a clean 12, take the next increment up (22.5 kg) and drop back to 8.
  4. That loop (add a rep, then add weight) is the entire progression model, for every exercise, indefinitely.

Early on your guesses will miss in both directions: one set dies at 6, another sails past 15. Correct the load next set or next session and carry on. Progress isn't linear either: some days the extra rep isn't there, other days you get two at once. The one hard rule: a rep that needs cheated form doesn't count.

Missed a day?

A skipped session is noise. Programmes fail from quitting, not from gaps; one missed day changes nothing as long as you pick the plan straight back up.

Option A: slide the workout to the next free day and push the rest of the week back one.

Option B: let it go, and do the next workout in the rotation on your next strength day.

Because the four workouts cycle through your Monday/Wednesday/Friday slots anyway, nothing is ever really lost; the sequence simply continues from wherever you are.

Chaotic week? If you only make the gym twice, do the next upper and the next lower in the sequence. Two sessions beats zero, and one rough week leaves no trace.

Section 3 · Couch to 5K

Why do cardio at all?

You could create the same calorie deficit by simply eating less, but dropping cardio would cost you:

  • A stronger heart and lungs, which shows up as better performance under the bar
  • Lower blood pressure and a healthier metabolism
  • Better insulin sensitivity, so what you eat is routed to muscle more readily
  • A lower resting heart rate, which tends to mean deeper, more restorative sleep

How it works

  • Nine phases over 14 weeks, adapted from the NHS Couch to 5K with a gentler build: phases 1–5 last two weeks each (do the week, then repeat it), phases 6–9 one week each.
  • Three sessions a week on your running days, strength on the days in between, one full rest day.
  • Every session starts and ends with a 5-minute walk.
  • The plan is built on time, not speed: if the pace feels too hard, slow down. A very slow, conversational jog counts.
  • Distances are rough estimates at 4.5 km/h walking and 7 km/h jogging.

Rules for moving up

  • Advance only when all three sessions are done with no new pain and normal recovery the next day
  • Hard but manageable? Repeat the phase before moving on
  • Never stack two sessions together to make up a missed one
  • The rest day stays a genuine rest day
  • With strength on the in-between days this is a six-day training week, so keep the strength work beginner-level and don't train to failure while you adapt
Stop the session if you feel dizzy, faint or unusually weak, or notice palpitations, chest pain or persistent nausea. If you're eating in a significant calorie deficit or have any medical conditions, check the plan with a clinician first.

Section 4 · So what now?

None of this needs memorising; the page isn't going anywhere. The whole programme compresses to a single instruction: start the week, and do the next session on the list.

Sunday: rest. Shop for groceries (protein, carbs, fats) and get a proper night's sleep

Monday: strength · Upper A. Warm up properly and find your starting weights

Tuesday: Couch to 5K

Wednesday: strength · Lower A

Thursday: Couch to 5K

Friday: strength · Upper B

Saturday: Couch to 5K

Next week your strength days carry on from where you left off: Monday is Lower B, then the cycle starts over with Upper A.
